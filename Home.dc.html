About

Hi, I'm Nat. I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Political Science at Columbia University, focusing on elite behavior in the American criminal-legal system. My dissertation project examines the human logic behind carceral outcomes: how police officers factor prosecutorial policy into arrest decisions, whether ever-greater volumes of digital evidence influence the speed and quality of prosecution, and the influence of selection and appointment systems on the sentencing decisions of judges. I care a lot about doing careful, causally-identified observational work that speaks to the everyday exercise of state power.

Before Columbia, I studied Political Science at Reed College and worked on political campaigns across the Pacific Northwest. Outside of working hours, I can often be found at one of New York’s many arthouse movie theaters or vegan restaurants.
